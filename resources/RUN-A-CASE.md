# Run a case trade

An original peer-practice guide. Use the case author's instructions when they differ. This is not a firm's interview policy or hiring rubric.

If this is your first time giving a case, start with an official guided sample such as McKinsey's Diconsa in the catalogue. In a **candidate-led** case, your partner decides which question or analysis to pursue, and you release the relevant information as they ask. In an **interviewer-led** case, you pose the source's questions in sequence, while your partner structures and solves each one. Both formats test independent thinking; neither requires the candidate to reproduce the author's exact framework.

## Before meeting

1. Agree who gives which case. Ask whether your partner has seen the title or a similar version; record exposure rather than treating a repeat as a cold attempt.
2. Open the **interviewer companion** and the complete source case. Read all prompts, clarifications, exhibits, solutions and conclusions. Recalculate the quantitative work yourself.
3. Prepare the exact exhibits you will share. Full casebooks and many prompt pages contain answers: never screen-share the whole PDF. Inspect each page visually before showing it. Export the approved candidate pages into separate private files, then share those files individually so scrolling cannot expose the solution.
4. Pick a mode: **coaching** permits teaching during the case; **practice interview** saves feedback for the end but allows appropriate hints; **benchmark** follows the agreed source instructions and records all assistance. Do not change modes silently.
5. Agree one learning target. Do not announce the case type or intended solution if discovering it is part of the exercise.

## A 60-minute slot

| Minutes | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | Ground rules, exposure check, prompt |
| 3–8 | Clarifications, quiet structuring pause, explanation of approach |
| 8–38 | Analysis, exhibits, calculations and discussion |
| 38–40 | Candidate recommendation: decision, evidence, risks, next step |
| 40–52 | Evidence-based feedback and comparison with the source |
| 52–60 | Redo one weak step; log one action for next time |

For 45 minutes, use 3 + 5 + 22 + 2 + 8 + 5 minutes. Follow source-specific timing where appropriate. A reciprocal trade usually needs two slots; do not squeeze two complete cases into one by removing feedback.

## During the case

- Give the prompt without explaining the framework. A deliberate quiet pause is fine; unexplained long stretches of calculation are less useful.
- Ask the candidate to explain the approach before calculating, then state the result's implication and next test. You do not need narration of every mental operation.
- Reveal source facts when the case's trigger is met. For candidate-led cases, relevant questions often trigger data; for interviewer-led cases, follow the question sequence. Do not force one preferred framework if another sound approach works.
- Distinguish **provided fact**, **candidate assumption**, and **unavailable information**. Never invent a missing number and present it as source data. If an assumption is needed, agree it explicitly and evaluate consistency with it.
- Use assistance proportionate to the chosen mode: 0 = none, 1 = neutral nudge, 2 = directional hint, 3 = worked step. Record help by phase. A factual clarification is not automatically a hint.
- If an error prevents useful progress, use the mode's agreed intervention, log it, and continue. Do not let a typo consume the entire session merely to preserve a score.
- If the packet is defective, stop using it as a benchmark. Mark the issue for repair and use another case or a clearly labelled drill.

## Close the loop

Use the [scorecard](SCORECARD.md). Identify one observed strength and at most two priorities, each with an example. Redo the weakest step immediately. Record the exact edition and case ID in a **private copy** of [SESSION-LOG.csv](SESSION-LOG.csv) and [ERROR-LOG.csv](ERROR-LOG.csv). Do not commit classmates' names, scores, recordings or feedback to this public repository.

The [packet template](CASE-PACKET-TEMPLATE.md) is for authoring or repairing a case. It is a blank template, not a finished interviewer version.
